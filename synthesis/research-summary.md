# Research summary and key insights

What the research added up to. The full findings are in [Desk Research](../research/Desk%20Research.md), [competitors](../research/competitors.md), the [Zendesk and Plain swimlanes](../research/workflow%20research/) and the [interview transcript](../research/Custom%20Support%20Interview%20Transcript.md).

## Research summary

- **Products** (Zendesk, Intercom, Gorgias, Plain, Sierra, Decagon): AI drafts and acts, but approvals are all-or-nothing, and the hand-off from AI to a person is the weakest moment.
- **Agents** rebuild context across tools on every ticket, and customers repeat what they already told the bot.
- **In India**, "delivered" is often the courier's word against the customer's, so the case turns on evidence.
- **Interview:** sending a reply closes the task while the real work is still open, so follow-ups live in a personal tracker.

## Key insights

1. **A reply isn't closure.** The ticket stays open until the customer knows the outcome.
2. **In delivery disputes, evidence decides:** photo, GPS, OTP.
3. **Flag risk privately.** Never accuse the customer.
4. **Leads need a live list of where to step in,** not reports.

## Where each insight shows up in JusDesk

| Insight | In the design |
|---|---|
| A reply isn't closure | Waiting statuses, AI follow-ups from the SLA, and closing only when the refund is confirmed |
| Evidence decides | The AI investigation card and Delivery details: courier, OTP, delivery proof, rider's GPS, nearby claims |
| Flag risk privately | Customer history signals that only the team sees; the AI never denies a claim |
| Leads need a live list | The Team Lead overview: "Needs you now" and "Patterns the AI spotted" |
